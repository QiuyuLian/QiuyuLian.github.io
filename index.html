About the lab

AI, mathematics and biology

Lian Lab is based at the University of Cambridge and works at the interface of artificial intelligence, dynamical systems and computational biology. We aim to understand the principles that govern cell fate decisions: how cellular identities emerge, how they are maintained, and how cells change state in response to developmental cues, tissue damage and disease.

We develop computational and mathematical approaches that move beyond static descriptions of cell populations towards predictive, mechanistic models of cellular dynamics. By integrating machine learning with single-cell and spatial omics, we seek to uncover the regulatory programmes and tissue interactions that shape cell behaviour over time.

Our research spans stem cell biology, tissue homeostasis and regeneration, cellular plasticity and early cancer evolution.

Our goal is to make cell fate dynamics understandable and predictable — and ultimately to identify how these processes can be redirected in disease.

Research directions

From cellular states to dynamic processes

We combine AI, mathematical modelling and multi-omics to reconstruct, understand and predict cell fate decisions.

01 / RECONSTRUCT

Reconstructing cell fate dynamics

Developing AI methods to infer cellular trajectories and state transitions from single-cell, multi-omic and spatial data across development, homeostasis, regeneration and disease.

02 / UNDERSTAND

Understanding the principles of cell fate decisions

Using dynamical systems, neural differential equations and topological methods to uncover the regulatory mechanisms and collective behaviours that shape cellular identity.

03 / PREDICT

Predicting and influencing cell fate

Integrating molecular and spatial information to predict cell-state transitions, identify key regulators and explore how cellular dynamics can be altered in disease.

  • Artificial intelligence and machine learning
  • Mathematical modelling and dynamical systems

  • Single-cell and spatial omics

Programming and quantitative skills are important for our research. We value curiosity, creativity and a genuine enthusiasm for developing computational approaches to address biological questions.
